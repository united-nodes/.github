# United Nodes of Anonymity (UNA)

The United Nodes of Anonymity (UNA) is a loose collective of developers, cryptographers, and researchers focused on applied cryptography and network privacy. 

This organization serves as an index for the source code, protocols, and technical documentation produced and utilized by this community. UNA is not a company, a foundation, or a formal institution. It has no management structure and operates entirely on the voluntary exchange of technical concepts and code.

## Authorship and Public Domain

All software and documentation hosted within this organization are released into the public domain. 

No individual, collective, or entity claims ownership, intellectual property rights, or administrative authority over these repositories. The tools contained herein were not manufactured by a central organization; they are the cumulative result of independent developers writing code. Because they belong to no one, they are available to everyone.

Within this framework, authorship is irrelevant. Code is evaluated solely on its mathematical integrity and functional security.

## Scope of Repositories

The community writes code to defend digital privacy. The repositories hosted here provide the foundational tools necessary to achieve that goal. While the specific implementations vary, the focus remains on:

*   **Applied Cryptography:** Low-level libraries and software for secure data encryption, key generation, and mathematical verification.
*   **Data Processing:** Utilities designed for handling, structuring, and verifying information without relying on centralized intermediaries or exposing plaintext.
*   **Privacy-Enhancing Tools:** Frameworks and software that allow individuals to communicate and operate on the network while maintaining strict control over their own data.

## Development Guidelines

Interaction with these repositories assumes a high level of technical proficiency and adherence to the principles of personal responsibility:

1.  **Code is the Mechanism:** We rely on code, not policy, to ensure privacy. Disagreements regarding architecture or implementation are expected and are typically resolved through forking the repository. 
2.  **No Technical Support:** The software is provided as-is, without warranties or safety nets. There is no customer service. Developers and users are solely responsible for their own operational security. Misuse of these tools may result in data compromise.
3.  **Strictly Voluntary:** Contributions are submitted, reviewed, and utilized on a strictly voluntary basis. There are no project roadmaps, issue bounties, or deployment deadlines.
